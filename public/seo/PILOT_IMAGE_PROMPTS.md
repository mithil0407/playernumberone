# Phase 2 pilot image prompts

These six prompts are written for the article visuals already registered in the site. Generate one **portrait 4:5 source image** per prompt, ideally **2048 × 2560 px or larger**. The site adds the ICONIK header and the explanatory labels in HTML, so the source image should contain **no embedded words, labels, arrows, lines, logos, or watermarks**.

Use the supplied ICONIK “Oversized / Intentional” image as a **design-language reference only**: cool blue-grey seamless studio, realistic Indian models, full-length fashion comparison, soft luxury lighting, restrained editorial colour, generous negative space, clean instructional composition. Do not copy its person, garments, or exact layout.

## 1. How to Look Taller in Clothes

**Article:** `/body-type-styling/how-to-look-taller-clothing`

**Prompt:**

Create a premium photorealistic fashion-intelligence editorial in a vertical 4:5 composition for an Indian personal-styling article about using clothing proportions to create a longer visual line. Show the same fictional short, curvy Indian woman twice, full body and facing camera, standing side by side on a cool blue-grey seamless studio floor. Preserve exactly the same face, hairstyle, skin tone, body size, height, shoulder width, waist, hips, leg length, camera distance, posture, and neutral confident expression on both sides; only the styling may change. The woman should appear around age 30–38, medium-brown Indian skin with natural texture, shoulder-length dark hair, understated makeup, and a relatable non-model body.

Left look: intentionally demonstrate an outfit that interrupts the vertical line—oversized charcoal-navy shirt with visibly dropped shoulder seams, excess width at the torso, low visual waist, long loose sleeves, dark wide trousers with fabric pooling over flat black shoes. The clothing must look plausible and high quality, not comedic or deliberately ugly. Right look: demonstrate intentional proportion—a rich deep-teal blouse with a clean shoulder seam, softly open neckline, sleeves ending near the forearm, blouse neatly tucked into high-rise warm taupe straight-to-wide-leg trousers, long uninterrupted trouser crease, hem almost touching pointed dark-brown shoes, small structured cognac handbag. The right styling should feel polished and achievable, not tight or body-altering.

Composition: leave the upper 18–20 percent calm and uncluttered for the website title overlay. Keep both full bodies and shoes fully inside the safe area, with equal visual weight and enough negative space on the far left and right for future crop flexibility. Place the left subject around 31 percent of the canvas width and the right around 69 percent. Use an eye-level 70–85 mm fashion-photography perspective with no wide-angle distortion. Soft large-source key light, gentle fill, subtle floor contact shadows, realistic fabric folds and tailoring, quiet luxury campaign finish, low-contrast blue-grey background, slate/teal/taupe/cognac palette. The difference must be understandable instantly as “broken, oversized proportions” versus “clean, intentional proportions,” while making it obvious that the woman’s body has not changed.

Avoid: slimming the right body, changing height or pose, glamour retouching, exaggerated hourglass shaping, stilettos, cropped feet, floating subjects, malformed hands, duplicate limbs, extra people, busy interiors, runway poses, text, typography, arrows, callout boxes, borders, logos, or watermarks.
