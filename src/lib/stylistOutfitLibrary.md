# ICONIK Stylist Outfit Library

Use this file to add outfit ideas that have worked well for real clients. The AI should use these as inspiration only: adapt the silhouette, coverage, colour, climate, budget, and formality to the current client. Do not copy a look exactly when it conflicts with the intake, body geometry, colour direction, or client preferences.

## How to Add an Outfit

Add one entry per outfit using this structure:

```
### Outfit Name
- Capsule: Professional | Social | Everyday | Occasion
- Occasion: Where this outfit works best
- Client type: Body, lifestyle, climate, or taste context where this succeeds
- Body-fit logic: Why the silhouette works
- Colour/palette notes: Palette direction and contrast level
- Pieces: Top; bottom or dress; outerwear; footwear; bag; jewellery/accessory
- Styling rationale: Why the full look feels coherent
- Adaptation rules: What can be changed for a different client
- Do not use when: Client profiles or constraints where this look should be avoided
```

## Outfit References

### Soft Authority Column
- Capsule: Professional
- Occasion: Office, client meetings, presentations, polished work travel
- Client type: Clients who want authority without looking severe; works well for medium to tall clients and anyone needing a longer vertical line.
- Body-fit logic: Tonal column under a structured third piece creates height and controls horizontal breaks. A gently shaped blazer gives waist definition without cling.
- Colour/palette notes: Use one deep neutral as the anchor, one softer neutral near the face, and one quiet accent in the bag or jewellery.
- Pieces: Fine knit or clean blouse; straight-leg trouser; single-breasted blazer; pointed flat or low block heel; structured shoulder bag; medium-scale earrings.
- Styling rationale: The look reads deliberate, senior, and repeatable because the silhouette is simple but the finish is sharp.
- Adaptation rules: Swap trousers for a column midi skirt for clients who prefer skirts. Use warmer neutrals for warm palettes and charcoal/navy for cool palettes.
- Do not use when: The client dislikes tailoring, needs very relaxed movement, or has strong coverage requirements that conflict with the neckline.

### Relaxed Smart Denim
- Capsule: Everyday
- Occasion: Coffee meetings, casual Fridays, errands where the client still wants polish
- Client type: Clients who default to casual clothes but want to stop looking unfinished.
- Body-fit logic: Straight denim balances the lower body without squeezing. A tucked or semi-tucked top and open layer create structure at the waist.
- Colour/palette notes: Use mid-depth denim with one clean neutral and one client-specific accent.
- Pieces: Fitted ribbed top or crisp tee; straight jeans; unstructured blazer or long cardigan; clean loafers or refined sneakers; soft tote; simple hoops or watch.
- Styling rationale: Familiar pieces become intentional through fit, proportion, and one elevated layer.
- Adaptation rules: For petite clients, keep the jacket shorter or use a vertical open front. For curvier clients, use denim with ease through hip and thigh.
- Do not use when: The client requested no denim or needs formal workplace clothing.

### Draped Dinner Ease
- Capsule: Social
- Occasion: Dinners, date nights, low-key celebrations
- Client type: Clients who want softness, movement, and a feminine line without bodycon styling.
- Body-fit logic: Draping creates shape without tightness. A defined shoulder or neckline keeps the outfit from collapsing visually.
- Colour/palette notes: Works best in one rich base colour with metallic or tonal accessories.
- Pieces: Draped wrap top or fluid blouse; bias midi skirt or wide-leg trouser; optional cropped jacket; heeled sandal or pointed mule; compact evening bag; luminous earrings.
- Styling rationale: The outfit feels dressed but comfortable because the drama comes from fabric movement instead of exposure.
- Adaptation rules: Raise neckline or add sleeves for modesty. Replace skirt with trouser for clients who prefer less leg focus.
- Do not use when: The client dislikes fluid fabrics or needs very structured body balance.

### Festive Modern Set
- Capsule: Occasion
- Occasion: Cultural events, festive dinners, family celebrations, wedding-adjacent events
- Client type: Clients who want Indian or modest occasion polish without heavy traditional styling.
- Body-fit logic: A clean longline layer and intentional neckline manage vertical proportion. Straight or softly flared bottoms keep the base elegant.
- Colour/palette notes: Use one festive colour from the client's accent palette, grounded with ivory, cocoa, navy, charcoal, or olive depending on undertone.
- Pieces: Structured kurta, tunic, or embellished blouse; straight pant, palazzo, or skirt; longline jacket or dupatta used vertically; embellished flats or low heels; clutch; statement earrings.
- Styling rationale: The look feels celebratory because texture and accessories carry the occasion while the silhouette remains controlled.
- Adaptation rules: Increase sleeve length, neckline coverage, or fabric opacity based on intake. Reduce embellishment for minimal clients.
- Do not use when: The client wants strictly Western outfits or dislikes festive detail.

### Travel Capsule Uniform
- Capsule: Everyday
- Occasion: Airports, sightseeing, long days, casual travel dinners
- Client type: Clients who need comfort but still want photos and public-facing moments to look composed.
- Body-fit logic: Soft structure prevents the outfit from reading like loungewear. A longer outer layer creates vertical flow.
- Colour/palette notes: Use stain-forgiving mid or deep neutrals with one scarf, bag, or shoe accent.
- Pieces: Breathable tee, knit polo, or soft shirt; pull-on straight trouser; lightweight overshirt or cardigan; supportive refined sneaker; crossbody or tote; scarf or sunglasses.
- Styling rationale: The outfit is practical because every piece moves, but the colour story and layer make it styled.
- Adaptation rules: Use linen blends in hot climates and fine merino or ponte in cool climates. Choose slip-on shoes if airport convenience matters.
- Do not use when: The client needs high-formality travel looks or dislikes sneakers.

### Quiet Evening Column
- Capsule: Social
- Occasion: Cocktail dinners, gallery events, upscale restaurants
- Client type: Clients with refined taste who prefer quiet impact over loud statement dressing.
- Body-fit logic: A clean column lengthens the body. One sculptural accessory creates focal control without disrupting the line.
- Colour/palette notes: Best in deep neutral, jewel tone, or monochrome tonal palette matched to depth and contrast.
- Pieces: Minimal dress or tonal top and skirt; sleek longline coat or cropped evening jacket; pointed heel or dressy flat; sculptural clutch; one strong earring or cuff.
- Styling rationale: The outfit looks expensive because it removes noise and focuses on proportion, fabric, and one precise accent.
- Adaptation rules: Add sleeves, a higher neckline, or a softer shoe depending on comfort and coverage. Use satin, crepe, or fine knit based on body-fit needs.
- Do not use when: The client wants expressive prints, high colour variety, or casual styling.
