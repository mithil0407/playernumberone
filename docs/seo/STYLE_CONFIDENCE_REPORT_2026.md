# Iconik Indian Style Confidence Report 2026

This document is the production specification for the original research asset. Do not publish findings until real responses have been collected and the founder approves the analysis.

## Research objective

Measure the practical wardrobe problems, confidence barriers, fit frustrations, colour uncertainty, and professional/occasion needs experienced by Iconik’s women’s and men’s Instagram audiences.

Women’s and men’s responses must remain separate in collection and reporting. Combined statistics may be shown only when the denominator and composition are disclosed.

## Collection method

- Recruitment: Instagram Stories and Reels from `iconik.style` and `iconik.men`.
- Collection window: 14 consecutive days.
- Eligibility: followers aged 18 or older who dress primarily for Indian climate, workplace, or occasion contexts.
- Responses: anonymous unless a participant separately opts into a research follow-up.
- Do not collect names, emails, phone numbers, exact addresses, body photographs, or health information in the research form.
- Store the raw export privately; publish aggregates only.
- Report exact response count for every chart and disclose skipped questions.
- Label the study as an audience survey, not a nationally representative Indian sample.

## Survey questions

Use identical core questions for women and men, followed by audience-specific wardrobe options.

1. How confident are you choosing an outfit without asking someone else? Scale 1–5.
2. Which decision consumes the most time? Fit, colour, coordination, occasion appropriateness, shopping, or repeating outfits.
3. How often do you buy something that looks good online but wrong when worn? Never, rarely, sometimes, often, almost always.
4. Which fit point fails most often? Shoulder, chest/bust, waist, stomach, hip, thigh, sleeve, garment length, or trouser length.
5. How certain are you about the colours that make your face look clearer? Scale 1–5.
6. Which wardrobe context creates the most stress? Everyday, work, interview, date/social, wedding, festive, travel, or formal event.
7. What is the most common reason an outfit remains unworn? Fit, colour, comfort, cannot coordinate, too formal, too casual, body changed, or purchase regret.
8. How many minutes do you usually need to decide what to wear? Under 5, 5–10, 11–20, 21–30, or more than 30.
9. Would a personal set of repeatable outfit rules make you more likely to use your existing wardrobe? Definitely not to definitely yes, scale 1–5.
10. One optional free-text question: What wardrobe problem do you wish someone could diagnose once and solve properly?

## Analysis rules

- Remove exact duplicates and clearly invalid responses; record how many were removed and why.
- Never invent missing responses or change denominators to make a finding stronger.
- Report counts alongside percentages.
- For scale questions, publish the full distribution, not only the mean.
- Cross-tab only groups with at least 30 valid responses; otherwise mark the result directional.
- Code free-text responses using a written codebook and retain an “other” category.
- Select findings because they are useful and supportable, not because they are sensational.

## Publication structure

1. Executive summary with five findings.
2. Methodology, field dates, recruitment source, sample sizes, limitations, and exclusions.
3. Women’s findings.
4. Men’s findings.
5. Shared wardrobe-friction findings.
6. Iconik interpretation: what brands, employers, stylists, and shoppers can do differently.
7. Downloadable chart pack and citation instructions.
8. Media contact and three short expert-commentary angles.

## Linkable chart set

- Confidence choosing an outfit.
- Most common fit failure.
- Purchase-regret frequency.
- Most stressful wardrobe context.
- Time spent deciding what to wear.
- Colour-confidence distribution.
- Women versus men comparison where question wording is identical.

Each chart needs a 16:9 press version, square social version, clear source line, response count, field dates, and accessible alt text.

## Outreach workflow

Build 150 qualified prospects across:

- Indian fashion and beauty publications.
- Women’s and men’s lifestyle editors.
- Workplace, leadership, HR, and career publications.
- Wedding and occasion publications.
- Indian diaspora publications.
- Consumer-behaviour and retail reporters.
- University fashion, textiles, psychology, and consumer-research departments.
- Newsletter writers and podcasters with a relevant editorial audience.

Qualification fields:

- Publication and domain.
- Contact name, role, and public contact route.
- Relevant article published in the previous 12 months.
- Audience fit: women, men, work, wedding, diaspora, retail, or research.
- Proposed finding or chart.
- Why the finding is relevant to that contact.
- Contact date, follow-up date, response, link, and status.

Automate discovery, prioritisation, and first-draft creation. A human must verify the contact, personalise the relevance line, and approve every send.
