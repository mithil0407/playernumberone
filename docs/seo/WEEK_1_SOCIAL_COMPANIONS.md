## 1. Oversized versus intentional silhouette

Tracked URL:

`https://www.iconik.pro/body-type-styling/how-to-look-taller-clothing?utm_source=instagram&utm_medium=organic_social&utm_campaign=seo_silhouette_week_1&utm_content=reel_oversized_intentional`

Reel script:

> Oversized is not the problem. Directionless volume is. Look at the first outfit. The shoulder seam drops, the sleeve pools, the waist disappears, and the trouser breaks over the shoe. Every construction point moves outward, so the eye reads width before it reads the person. Now look at the second outfit. We did not make it tight. We gave the shoulder a location, placed the waist deliberately, opened the neckline, and let the trouser fall without pooling. Same body. Same need for comfort. But now the eye has one clean route through the outfit. The goal is not to look smaller. The goal is to make every garment line intentional.

Caption hook: `You do not need tighter clothes. You need clearer construction points.`

Poll: `What causes more trouble in your wardrobe: dropped shoulders or pooled trousers?`

Comment prompt: `Write TORSO or LEGS and tell us where outfits usually feel too long.`

## 2. Dusky skin is not an undertone

Tracked URL:

`https://www.iconik.pro/colour-analysis/best-colours-dusky-skin?utm_source=instagram&utm_medium=organic_social&utm_campaign=seo_colour_week_1&utm_content=reel_dusky_undertone`

Reel script:

> If someone gives every dusky woman the same colour palette, they are skipping the most important variable. Dusky describes visible skin depth. It does not tell us whether the undertone is warm, cool, neutral, or olive. That is why one person looks clear in terracotta and another looks heavy in it, even when their surface skin depth looks similar. Start with controlled drapes in the same daylight. Compare warm cream with clean white, terracotta with berry, and olive with emerald. Watch the face, not the fabric. Does the colour soften shadows and sharpen the eyes, or does it add yellow, grey, or redness? Your skin is not difficult. The category was incomplete.

Caption hook: `“Dusky” is a depth description, not a colour prescription.`

Poll: `Which usually looks better near your face: ivory or crisp white?`

Comment prompt: `Comment GOLD, SILVER, or BOTH—then test the answer with fabric, not jewellery alone.`

## 3. Dark skin and colour intensity

Tracked URL:

`https://www.iconik.pro/colour-analysis/dark-skin-colour-guide-india?utm_source=instagram&utm_medium=organic_social&utm_campaign=seo_colour_week_1&utm_content=reel_deep_skin_contrast`

Reel script:

> Dark skin does not need one list of “bold colours.” It needs the right relationship between temperature, clarity, and contrast. A clear cobalt can frame cool deep skin beautifully, while a warmer complexion may come alive beside coral, rust, olive, or warm cream. And pastels are not automatically forbidden. A pastel fails when it removes too much definition near the face, not because it belongs to someone else’s skin depth. Test the colour in consistent daylight. If the face loses clarity, try a clearer version, a deeper neckline, or your strongest jewellery metal. Do not judge the colour in isolation. Judge what it makes the face do.

Caption hook: `Deep skin can wear pastels. The question is which pastel—and what supports it.`

Poll: `Do very pale colours make you feel clear or washed out?`

Comment prompt: `Name the colour people told you to avoid.`

## 4. Wheatish skin meaning

Tracked URL:

`https://www.iconik.pro/colour-analysis/best-colours-wheatish-skin-india?utm_source=instagram&utm_medium=organic_social&utm_campaign=seo_colour_week_1&utm_content=reel_wheatish_meaning`

Reel script:

> “Wheatish” is one of India’s most common skin descriptions—and one of the least useful colour diagnoses. It describes a medium beige-to-brown surface depth, often with a golden or olive appearance. But two wheatish complexions can react completely differently to the same fabric. One becomes clearer beside terracotta and cream. Another needs navy, berry, emerald, or clean white. A third sits between warm and cool and needs muted, balanced colours. So do not ask, “What suits wheatish skin?” Ask three better questions: what is the undertone, how much contrast does the face carry, and how clear or muted should the colour be?

Caption hook: `Wheatish is the beginning of the test, not the result.`

Poll: `Do you describe your skin as warm, olive, neutral, or unsure?`

Comment prompt: `Comment WHEATISH if colour lists keep contradicting one another.`

## 5. Modest does not mean oversized

Tracked URL:

`https://www.iconik.pro/style-guides/modest-professional-fashion-india?utm_source=instagram&utm_medium=organic_social&utm_campaign=seo_workwear_week_1&utm_content=reel_modest_structure`

Reel script:

> Many women add coverage by buying a larger garment. That changes more than coverage. The shoulder drops, the sleeve loses structure, the waist disappears, and the hem adds volume at the widest point. Modest dressing becomes polished when coverage comes from design instead. Keep the shoulder accurate. Choose an opaque breathable fabric. Decide the sleeve and hem length deliberately. Then locate the waist with a seam, tuck, tonal layer, or clean vertical opening. A straight kurta with tonal trousers and a structured blouse with wide-leg trousers use the same logic. Coverage stays. Authority returns.

Caption hook: `Coverage should come from construction, not an accidental size increase.`

Poll: `Which is harder for office wear: heat, coverage, or fit?`

Comment prompt: `Comment ETHNIC or WESTERN for the next formula breakdown.`
